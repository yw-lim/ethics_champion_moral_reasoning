Moral Reasoning & Governance Field Guide

Core Philosophical Frameworks

Utilitarianism (Consequentialism)

Pioneered by Jeremy Bentham and John Stuart Mill. Determines morality by calculating net consequences: the greatest good for the greatest number of stakeholders.

Deontology (Kantian Duty)

Formulated by Immanuel Kant. Morality is based on duties, rights, and categorical imperatives. Actions are inherently right or wrong regardless of outcomes.

Virtue Ethics

Originating from Aristotle. Focuses on moral character and practical wisdom. Asks: "What kind of professional officer would I become if I take this action?"

Justice & Fairness (Rawls)

John Rawls's "Veil of Ignorance". Ensures fair distribution of risks and rewards, protecting the most vulnerable stakeholders in financial decisions.

IESBA Code of Ethics for Accountants

  • Integrity: Straightforwardness and honesty in all professional and business relationships.
  • Objectivity: Not allowing bias, conflict of interest, or undue influence to override judgments.
  • Professional Competence & Due Care: Maintaining professional knowledge and skill at required standards.
  • Confidentiality: Respecting the confidentiality of information acquired during duties.
  • Professional Behavior: Complying with relevant laws and avoiding actions that discredit the profession.
